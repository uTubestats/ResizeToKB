How to Resize an Image to Exact Pixel Dimensions (Without Stretching It)

Updated October 2026

Social platforms, forms, and websites often ask for an image at exact pixel dimensions, such as 1080 × 1350 or 413 × 531. Getting those numbers right is easy. Getting them without distorting the picture takes one extra step, which this guide explains.

What pixels are

A pixel is a single dot of an image. The width and height in pixels tell you how many dots there are across and down. More pixels means more detail and a larger file, but not necessarily a larger picture on screen.

Aspect ratio in one minute

Aspect ratio is the shape of the picture: width divided by height. A square is 1:1, and a typical phone photo is about 4:3. If your target has a different shape from your photo, you have two options: crop the photo to fit, or stretch it. Stretching makes faces look wide or tall, so cropping is nearly always better.

Steps to resize to exact pixels

  1. Open the resize image by pixels tool.
  2. Select your photo.
  3. Type the width. With Keep ratio on, the height follows automatically.
  4. If both numbers must match exactly, switch Keep ratio off. Check that the shape is close to the original first, or crop the photo beforehand.
  5. Click Resize Image and download the result.

Use a preset when one exists

Presets set the size and crop to fit in one go, so nothing is stretched. Try the pages for Instagram posts, Instagram Stories, YouTube thumbnails, WhatsApp profile pictures, and passport photos.

Pixels versus DPI

DPI, or dots per inch, only matters when printing. It does not change the number of pixels. For example, a 413 × 531 pixel image prints at 35 × 45 mm at 300 DPI. Online forms care about pixels, so ignore DPI unless you print.

Tips for a sharp result

  • Resize down rather than up. Enlarging stretches existing pixels and softens the image.
  • Keep your original and make each size from it.
  • Check the final size on screen before uploading.
  • If a form also has a KB limit, set the pixels first and the KB second.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my image look stretched after resizing?

The new width and height had a different shape from the original, and Keep ratio was off. Crop first, or leave Keep ratio on.

Can I set only the width?

Yes. With Keep ratio on, type the width and the height is worked out for you.

Does changing pixels change the file size in KB?

Yes. Fewer pixels usually means a smaller file, though the format and detail also matter. For an exact KB target, use the KB tool.
