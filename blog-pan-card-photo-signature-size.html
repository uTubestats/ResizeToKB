PAN Card Photo and Signature Size in KB and Pixels

Updated October 2026

Few people find one clear answer for the PAN card photo size online. Different websites list different numbers, and that is a real problem when an upload form rejects your file. This guide shows what the numbers are, why they differ, and how to make sure your files match the portal you are using.

What the printed photo looks like

Guides agree that the printed PAN photo is about 3.5 cm by 2.5 cm (35 × 25 mm), in colour, with a white or light plain background and a clearly visible face. The online upload rules are where the numbers start to differ.

These are the figures that appear in published guides, not an official rule. The portal's own instructions, shown on the upload screen, are the final word, and NSDL (Protean) and UTIITSL can differ.

How to find your exact limit

  1. Start the application on the NSDL or UTIITSL site and go to the photo upload step.
  2. Read the size, format, and dimension rules shown on that screen.
  3. Write down the pixel size and the KB range.

How to resize your PAN photo and signature

  1. Open the pixels tool and enter the exact width and height from the portal. Switch off Keep ratio if both values must match, and crop the photo first.
  2. Run the result through the reduce image size in KB tool and type the portal's maximum.
  3. If the portal also sets a minimum and your file is below it, use the increase image size in KB tool.
  4. For the signature, sign on white paper with black ink, crop closely, and repeat the same steps.

Common reasons a PAN upload fails

  • The file is outside the KB range, either too large or too small.
  • The photo is not a JPG, or it is a screenshot.
  • The background is dark, patterned, or full of shadows.
  • The signature touches the edge of the frame or is too pale.

Step by step: from camera to upload

  1. Take a recent colour photo against a plain white wall, in daylight, with your face centred and your eyes open.
  2. Crop it to the shape the portal asks for. A square crop is common for online uploads.
  3. Use the pixels tool to set the exact pixel size shown on the upload screen.
  4. Use the KB tool to bring the file under the maximum.
  5. Open the final file, zoom in, and check your face and the edges of the signature before you upload.

NSDL and UTIITSL: why the instructions differ

PAN applications are handled through more than one service provider, and each can show its own upload screen with its own size, format, and dimension limits. A guide written for one portal can therefore be wrong for the other. When two sources disagree, the screen you are looking at wins.

Photo checklist

  • A recent photo, not a photo of a printed photo.
  • No shadows on the face or the wall behind you.
  • No cap, sunglasses, or heavy filters.
  • The file is a JPG with a clear name, such as pan-photo.jpg.

Signature checklist

  • Blue or black ink on plain white paper.
  • The signature fits inside the frame without touching the edges.
  • The file is a JPG and meets the size and pixel limits shown by the portal.

Frequently asked questions

What is the PAN card photo size in KB?

Guides list different ranges, such as 20 to 50 KB or 10 to 200 KB, so check the portal's upload screen and use the number shown there.

What pixel size is a PAN card photo?

213 × 213 pixels appears in many guides for online uploads, but the portal's instructions decide. The printed photo is about 3.5 × 2.5 cm.
