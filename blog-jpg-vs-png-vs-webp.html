JPG vs PNG vs WebP: Which Image Format Should You Use?

Updated October 2026

Picking the right format can cut a file size a lot without any visible loss. Here is a quick guide to the three formats you will meet most often.

Quick comparison

JPGPNGWebP
Best forPhotosLogos, text, screenshotsWebsites
CompressionLossyLosslessLossy or lossless
TransparencyNoYesYes
File sizeSmallLargeSmallest

When to use JPG

JPG is the safe choice for photos. Almost every form, portal and app accepts it, and you can lower the quality to hit a size such as 50KB or 100KB. It is the format most exam forms ask for.

When to use PNG

PNG keeps sharp edges and supports transparent backgrounds, which suits logos, icons and screenshots with text. Files are larger, and because PNG is lossless you cannot shrink it to an exact KB limit by lowering quality. To reduce a PNG, make the pixel size smaller or convert it to JPG.

When to use WebP

WebP usually gives smaller files than JPG at similar quality and supports transparency. It is a good choice for your own website. Some older forms and apps do not accept it, so use JPG when uploading to a form.

Which should you pick?

  • Uploading to a form or portal: JPG.
  • Logo or graphic with a transparent background: PNG.
  • Images on your own website: WebP.

Format myths

  • "PNG is always higher quality." PNG is lossless, but for a photo the extra quality is invisible and the file can be many times larger.
  • "WebP works everywhere." Modern browsers support it, but some older apps, forms, and email tools do not.
  • "JPG always looks bad." At a sensible quality setting, JPG photos look excellent. Visible blocks appear only when the quality is pushed very low.

Frequently asked questions

Does converting PNG to JPG reduce quality?

Slightly, because JPG is lossy, but for photos the difference is usually hard to see. Transparent areas become a solid color, usually white.

Which format is best for SEO?

Smaller files load faster, which helps page speed. WebP or well-compressed JPG are good choices for website photos.

Which format should I use for exam forms?

JPG, unless the form states another format.

A simple workflow

Keep your original photo untouched. Make a resized JPG for forms, a WebP copy for your website, and a PNG only when you need transparency. This way you never lose quality by repeatedly compressing the same file.
