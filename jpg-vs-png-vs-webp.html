JPG vs PNG vs WebP: Which Format Is Smallest?

Published 25 October 2026 · by ResizeToKB

JPG vs PNG vs WebP is a common question when you need a small file. The short answer is that photos are usually smallest as WebP or JPG, and flat graphics are often best as PNG.

JPG vs PNG vs WebP at a Glance

FormatBest forTransparent backgroundSize of photos
JPGPhotosNoSmall
PNGLogos, screenshots, textYesLarge
WebPPhotos and graphicsYesOften smaller than JPG

Which Format Should You Choose?

  • Choose JPG for photos on forms and portals. Almost every site accepts it.
  • Choose PNG for logos, screenshots and images with sharp text.
  • Choose WebP when the site accepts it and you want the smallest file.

Some older forms and programs do not accept WebP, so check the rules first.

How to Change Format and Size

  1. Open the image resizer and select your picture.
  2. Choose how to resize: pixels, percentage or Target KB.
  3. Pick JPG, WebP or PNG in the Save as list.
  4. Press Resize Image and compare the file size shown in the result.

In Target KB mode, a PNG is saved as JPG so the tool can reach your size.

Lossy and Lossless Compression

JPG uses lossy compression. It throws away small details that the eye rarely notices, which makes files small. PNG is lossless, so it keeps every detail but needs more space for photos. WebP can work in both ways.

Examples

  • A holiday photo for a job portal: JPG.
  • A screenshot with text for a guide: PNG.
  • A product photo for a fast website: WebP, if your platform supports it.
  • A logo with a see-through background: PNG or WebP.

Support Across Websites and Forms

Modern browsers show WebP well. Some older forms, email tools and desktop programs may still reject it. When a form lists the allowed formats, choose one from that list. If it does not say, JPG is the safest choice.
